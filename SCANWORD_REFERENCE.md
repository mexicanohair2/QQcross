# QQCross scanword reference specification

## Official interactive generator reference

- **Kryss Crossword Maker:** https://kryss.app/maker
- This is the project-approved maker/editor reference for grid setup and text/picture clue authoring. The public page describes choosing grid size, language and difficulty, then writing text or picture clues and playing/sharing/printing the result.
- The live maker reports a default grid of **8 across × 12 down** and a maximum of 40 × 40. Its visible workflow is Grid → Clues → Play, Share, Print.
- The maker's default 8×12 size agrees with QQCross's fixed production contract. QQCross must remain **8 columns × 12 rows = 96 cells**; never import the maker's configurable size or maximum into QQCross. Its page also reports a configurable maximum of 40 × 40, which is not relevant to QQCross.
- Use the maker as a behavioral/structural reference only. QQCross must keep its own Russian dictionary, questions, answers, artwork, scoring, and implementation.

## Observed maker behavior and verification limits

- The public maker page embeds its canvas from `https://crosswordtool-canvas.vercel.app/`.
- The extracted canvas text says **“Drag to create your grid”**. Its metadata describes a drag-and-drop crossword editor.
- This supports using the page as a grid-editor and clue-authoring reference. It does **not** establish that the embedded tool automatically arranges answers or validates crossings for the user.
- A live browser interaction with the canvas could not be completed in this session. Do not claim that its automatic placement, clue-validation, or crossing-validation behavior has been inspected. Until those dynamic controls are directly observed, it is not a verified algorithm-quality benchmark.

## Visual reference asset

Reference screenshot (Dropbox): `/Screenshot_20261105_010300_app_kryds_android_MessagingUnityPlayerActivity.jpg`

The saved screenshot is a visual reference for cell proportions, board emphasis, clue-cell treatment, direction indicators, and the overall game-screen composition. Its captured board is 13 × 13; that screenshot dimension is **not** QQCross's board dimension and must not override the agreed 8×12 contract.

## Confirmed visual structure

- Square cells with a small, consistent gap.
- The grid is the main element in the middle of the game screen.
- The player/status header and scores sit above the grid.
- The letter rack and check/confirm control sit below the grid.
- Clue cells appear on the top and left edges; top-edge clues start downward, left-edge clues start to the right.
- Direction indicators are small and point right or down in the inspected screenshot.
- The clue text/content is generated by QQCross and must remain our own; the reference controls the visual structure and placement, not the actual clue wording.

## Implementation guardrails

- Never change the agreed **8×12** dimensions.
- Keep the board large and centered in the available phone game area; it must not shrink to a small tile or force horizontal/vertical scrolling.
- Preserve the visual clue-cell language, small direction indicators, and vertical composition from the screenshot.
- The current production topology is validated for right/down placements. Do not enable left/up production placements until topology, CSP, validator, renderer, hints, and all four direction paths pass the same independent tests.
- Keep clue contents generated from QQCross data; do not copy the reference game's questions or answers.
