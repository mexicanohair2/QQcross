# QQCross scanword reference specification

## Official interactive maker/editor reference

- **Kryss Crossword Maker:** https://kryss.app/maker
- This is the project-approved maker/editor reference for grid setup and text/picture clue authoring. The public page describes choosing grid size, language and difficulty, then writing text or picture clues and playing/sharing/printing the result.
- The live maker reports a default grid of **8 across × 12 down** and a maximum of 40 × 40. Its visible workflow is Grid → Clues → Play, Share, Print.
- The maker's default 8×12 size agrees with QQCross's fixed production contract. QQCross must remain **8 columns × 12 rows = 96 cells**; never import the maker's configurable size or maximum into QQCross. Its page also reports a configurable maximum of 40 × 40, which is not relevant to QQCross.
- Use the maker as a behavioral/structural reference only. QQCross must keep its own Russian dictionary, questions, answers, artwork, scoring, and implementation.

## Observed maker behavior and verification limits

- The public maker page embeds its canvas from `https://crosswordtool-canvas.vercel.app/`.
- The extracted canvas text says **“Drag to create your grid”**. Its metadata describes a drag-and-drop crossword editor.
- This supports using the page as a grid-editor and clue-authoring reference. It does **not** establish that the embedded tool automatically arranges answers or validates crossings for the user.
- A live browser interaction with the canvas could not be completed in this session. Do not claim that its automatic placement, clue-validation, or crossing-validation behavior has been inspected. Until those dynamic controls are directly observed, it is not a verified algorithm-quality benchmark.

## Puzzle-construction quality reference — automatic scanword generator

- **MyToolkit automatic scanword generator:** https://mytoolkit.ru/scanword
- The public page describes entering answer/clue pairs and automatically placing words with intersections in seconds.
- Its stated output uses clue cells inside the grid, with arrows showing answer direction (right or down).
- The page says it aims to place all submitted words; words it cannot place are reported separately rather than silently inserted as broken entries.
- Its stated practical range is about 8–30 words. This is a quality/behavior benchmark, not a geometry contract: QQCross remains fixed at 8×12 (96 cells), uses its own Russian dictionary, and must independently validate every generated board.
- The public description was inspected, but an interactive sample puzzle was not generated in this session. Treat the advertised behavior as the benchmark specification, not as a claim that its internal algorithm or actual output has been exhaustively tested.
- QQCross must meet the same visible structural expectations while applying stricter game-specific rules: no orphan question/letter cells, no unregistered answer slots, no conflicting crossings, no silent dropped answers, no repeated answers, five distinct picture clue cells, and independent validation before gameplay.

- The letter rack and check/confirm control sit below the grid.

- The clue text/content is generated by QQCross and must remain our own; the reference controls the visual structure and placement, not the actual clue wording.

## Current production layout refinement

- Keep the canonical **engine/data grid at 8 columns × 12 rows = 96 cells**. The production adapter `scanword-engine/src/legacy-adapter.js` rotates that certified model for the existing game renderer, which displays a **12-column × 8-row wide board**. This is a presentation transform only: do not transpose or resize the generator's 8×12 source grid.
- Put three main clue anchors on the top row at columns 1, 4 and 7 (the top-left corner remains blank).
- Keep the left-edge clue cells in rows 1–11 and at least six clue cells in the interior.
- Do not use the entire rightmost column as repeated clue/terminal cells. The normal topology uses one lower-right terminal cell to close the long vertical answer; the rare eleven-letter layout may retain only its own validated sparse edge structure.
- At common phone sizes, the board width must be at least 85% of the viewport where the available height permits it. Keep the complete board and letter/tool controls inside the viewport without horizontal scrolling.

## Implementation guardrails

- Never change the agreed **8×12** dimensions.
- Keep the rotated 12×8 play surface large and centered in the available phone game area; it must not shrink to a small tile or force horizontal scrolling. Preserve the adapter's coordinate and direction transform for clue cells, answer cells, hints and terminal positions.
- Preserve the visual clue-cell language, small direction indicators and compact composition from the screenshot. The screenshot's visual cues apply to the source topology; the playable board is rendered wide by the documented adapter rotation.
- QQCross supports all four answer directions: right, left, down and up. Each placement must pass topology, CSP, assembler, independent validator, renderer and hint-path checks. The screenshot's visible arrows are a visual reference, not a restriction on production directions.
- Keep clue contents generated from QQCross data; do not copy the reference game's questions or answers.

## Engine geometry and play-screen orientation

- The independent generator and validator use a 12-row × 8-column model (`width: 8`, `height: 12`). This remains the only valid topology format.
- `toLegacyPuzzle()` in `scanword-engine/src/legacy-adapter.js` rotates model coordinates and directions and emits `w: 12`, `h: 8` for the game renderer. The rendered board therefore has 12 columns × 8 rows and a 3:2 aspect ratio.
- Keep tests explicit about which layer they validate: generator/topology tests assert 8×12; browser rendering tests assert the rotated 12×8 surface. Never “fix” one layer by changing the other.
